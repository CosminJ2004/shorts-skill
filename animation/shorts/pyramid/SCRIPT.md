## Facts and sources
(Research collected by the shorts-studio documentary job `hidden-history`, research.json.)
- The Great Pyramid of Cholula (Tlachihualtepetl, "man-made mountain") in Puebla, Mexico, is the largest pyramid on Earth
  by volume, ~4.45 million m3, nearly twice the Great Pyramid of Giza (~2.5 million m3). Guinness World Records;
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Cholula
- Building began around the 3rd-2nd century BC; it was enlarged in stages (4 major, 9 minor) over ~1,000 years, each
  new pyramid built over the old one. Discover Magazine; Wikipedia.
- Building stopped around AD 700-800 as the city declined; the pyramid overgrew and looked like a natural hill.
- In 1594 the Spanish built the church of Our Lady of Remedies (Nuestra Senora de los Remedios) on the summit.
- In 1931 archaeologist/architect Ignacio Marquina began tunnelling; ~8 km of tunnels by 1954; they follow older pyramids
  inside the newer ones.
- The "Mural of the Drinkers": ~56 m long, ~110 figures drinking (likely pulque), found 1969.
- Most of the pyramid has never been excavated; the church on top is a protected monument.

## Script (~205 words, ~3.6 words/s)
The biggest pyramid on Earth isn't in Egypt. It's hiding under this church in Mexico.
Over two thousand years ago, the people of Cholula built a temple on a flat plain.
A few generations later, it felt too small.
So instead of tearing it down, they built a bigger pyramid right on top of it.
Then their grandkids did the same thing. And their grandkids. For almost a thousand years.
By the end, it held nearly twice as much as the Great Pyramid of Giza.
Then the city shrank, and nobody looked after it.
Rain washed dirt down the steps, seeds sprouted, and trees moved in.
Until the biggest pyramid on the planet looked exactly like a hill.
So in 1594, the Spanish built a church on top.
Luckily, that meant nobody tore it down.
Unluckily, it also means nobody can dig it up.
You see, the pyramid is built like nesting dolls. Open one, and there's an older one inside.
In 1931, archaeologists started tunnelling in, and dug about eight kilometres of tunnels.
They found stairs inside stairs, and a mural fifty metres long of people at a drinking party.
And most of it still hasn't been dug up.
So the biggest pyramid on Earth is still mostly a hill, wearing a church as a hat.
