# "Doomsday Is Today": script v1 (2026-11-13)

This is the cartoon-3d-short format, iteration 7: about 55-60 s, vertical, in English, with a directed expressive
narrator.

The topic is viral right now. The 1960 *Science* paper "Doomsday: Friday, 13 November, A.D. 2026" is all over the news
this week, because the date is today.

## Facts and sources
- **The paper.** Heinz von Foerster, Patricia M. Mora and Lawrence W. Amiot, "Doomsday: Friday, 13 November, A.D.
  2026", *Science* vol. 132, no. 3436, 4 November 1960, pp. 1291-1295.
  - Subtitle: at this date human population will approach infinity if it grows as it has grown in the last two
    millennia.
  - [ADS record](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1960Sci...132.1291V/abstract)
- **Method.** They fitted 24 world population estimates from the past 2,000 years. The doubling time shrinks to zero on
  that date, so the population approaches infinity.
  - [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/scientists-once-calculated-the-date-of-doomsday-and-its-next-month-2000820261)
  - [Wikipedia: Heinz von Foerster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_von_Foerster)
- **"They will be squeezed to death."** A quote from the paper (Gizmodo).
- **The birthday.** Von Foerster was born on 13 November 1911 in Vienna (Wikipedia). The date is his 115th birthday,
  and sources say the date was most likely a joke.
  - [IFLScience](https://www.iflscience.com/an-old-paper-really-did-suggest-a-mathematical-doomsday-on-13-november-2026-but-theres-no-need-to-worry-84777)
  - [Sciencing](https://www.sciencing.com/2138206/how-1960-physicist-predicted-world-end-november-13-2026/)
- **Stage magician.** Gizmodo says he was, at one point, a stage magician. Wikipedia mentions his interest in magic.
- **Reception.** Mathematicians and demographers wrote letters to *Science* calling the paper "ridiculous" and
  "manifestly absurd", and the authors defended their maths (Gizmodo). Time and The New York Times covered the paper.
- **Accuracy.** The predictions held until around 1993, and the equation was more accurate than most demographers'
  projections in the 1970s and 1980s (Gizmodo).
- **Why it was wrong.** Birth rates fell. The global birth rate today is slightly less than half its 1960 level
  (Gizmodo).
- **Today.** About 8.32 billion people (Worldometer, UN-based: 8,324,412,938 on 3 November 2026), growing about 0.84 %
  a year.
  - [Worldometer](https://www.worldometers.info/world-population/)

## Catchy checklist (user feedback, iteration 6: "very catchy, a real hook")
- **Hook in the first 2 s:** the exact day humanity ends... and it's this date. A paradox plus an open loop: are we
  doomed?
- **Credibility flip at 6 s:** "Not a cult. Not a psychic. The journal Science."
- **A reveal or joke every 3-5 s:**
  - infinity;
  - squeezed to death;
  - his birthday;
  - he was a magician;
  - "ridiculous";
  - and yet he was right for 30 years.
- **"You see," pivot:** the maths wasn't the problem; people were.
- **Analogy:** a car racing at a cliff that hits the brakes.
- **Loop ending:** "Happy birthday, Heinz" pays off the birthday. The last line ("if you're watching this...") leads
  back to the hook.

## Script
In 1960, three scientists calculated the exact day humanity ends.
Friday. November thirteenth. 2026.
Not a cult. Not a psychic. The journal Science.
They took two thousand years of population numbers, and spotted something terrifying.
Every time humanity doubled, it doubled faster.
Follow that curve, and on one Friday, the number of humans hits infinity.
Their verdict? We wouldn't starve. We'd be squeezed to death.
And the date they picked for doomsday? The lead scientist's birthday.
Oh, and he used to be a stage magician.
Other scientists called it ridiculous.
But for thirty years, the curve was right. More right than the experts.
You see, the math wasn't the problem. People were.
Families started having fewer kids. The birth rate fell to about half.
Like a car racing at a cliff, that finally hits the brakes.
So instead of infinity, there are about eight billion of us.
Which means, if you're watching this, the world didn't end.
Happy birthday, Heinz.
