# "The Castle That Fell Through Its Toilet": script v1 (2026-11-11)

This is the cartoon-3d-short format: 2D cartoon cards in a 3D toon world, about 65 s, vertical, one steady
narrator, in English.

## Facts and sources

**Construction.** Richard I (the Lionheart) built Château Gaillard on a chalk cliff above the Seine at Les Andelys,
Normandy, from 1196 to about 1198. That is about two years, unusually fast for a large stone castle.
- It had three wards, one behind the other, and ditches cut into the rock. The inner ward is reached over a rock bridge.
- [Wikipedia: Château Gaillard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teau_Gaillard)

**Cost.** The building cost about £12,000. Richard spent about £7,000 on castles in England over his whole reign, and
Dover cost about £7,000 between 1179 and 1191.
- Source: Wikipedia (Château Gaillard).

**Richard's quotes.**
- "Behold, how fair is this year-old daughter of mine!" (Wikipedia)
- He said he could hold it "were the walls made of butter". The chronicles pair it with Philip II: "If its walls were
  made of solid iron, yet would I take them!" The exchange is often called probably apocryphal, so the script says
  "said" in a storytelling voice.
- [Medieval Substack: The Last Castle](https://medieval.substack.com/p/man-of-a-thousand-castles-the-last)
- [Dan Jones: I can't believe it's not butter](https://danjones.substack.com/p/i-cant-believe-its-not-butter)

**Richard's death.** He died on 6 April 1199. John became king and inherited the castle (Wikipedia).

**The chapel.** John had a chapel built in the middle ward, with a garderobe (latrine) beside it that needed an opening
in the ward wall.
- [Weapons and Warfare](https://weaponsandwarfare.com/?p=20640)
- Harper's New Monthly Magazine, v.109 (it says John added a large chapel, with a smaller building beside it).

**The siege.** Philip II besieged the castle from August/September 1203 to 6 March 1204.
- The outer ward fell after miners dug under its advanced tower.
- The middle ward fell when "Philip's men climbed up a garderobe (toilet chute) and entered the chapel above". They
  set fires, and in the confusion opened the gates.
- [Wikipedia: Siege of Château Gaillard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Ch%C3%A2teau_Gaillard)
- [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/223513): the French "penetrated the main fortress through the
  latrines".
- Some accounts say the way in was the chapel window instead. The script follows the latrine account (Britannica,
  Wikipedia).

**The end.** The inner ward fell soon after. 20 knights and 120 soldiers surrendered on 6 March 1204. By June 1204,
John had lost almost all of Normandy (Rouen surrendered on 24 June).

**Medieval toilets.** A garderobe was a stone seat over a shaft or chute that dropped down and out through the wall.
- [World History Encyclopedia: Toilets in a Medieval Castle](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/1239/toilets-in-a-medieval-castle)

**Jokes.** Every joke is a real fact:
- "nobody guarded the toilet": that is how they got in;
- "the whole castle had been using it for six months": the garrison was besieged from autumn 1203.

## Format check
- **Hook:** sentence 1, 14 words (target 10-16).
- **Setting, pride and escalation:** sentences 2-9, ending in the plant: "a chapel, with a toilet".
- **Snap:** sentences 12-14.
- **"Luckily / Unluckily":** sentences 15-16.
- **"You see," pivot and everyday analogy:** sentences 17-18 (the bank vault with a doggy door).
- **Consequence:** sentences 19-20.
- **Callback twist:** sentences 21-22 bring back the butter boast.
- **Characters:** Richard (pride), Philip (rival), John (comic heir), the French soldier (hero of the snap), defenders,
  workers and miners.

## Script
The strongest castle in France was captured by soldiers who climbed up its toilet.
In 1196, Richard the Lionheart built it on a cliff above the River Seine.
Three rings of walls, on solid rock, built in just two years.
It cost twelve thousand pounds, more than all his castles in England combined.
Richard was so proud, he called it his one-year-old daughter.
The king of France said, "I'd take it, even if its walls were made of iron."
Richard said, "I'd hold it, even if they were made of butter."
Then Richard died, and his brother John got the castle.
John's big addition? A chapel. With a toilet.
In 1203, the French arrived and dug under the first wall until it collapsed.
But the second wall held.
Until one soldier looked up, and spotted a hole in the wall.
The toilet chute.
He climbed all the way up, straight into the chapel.
Luckily for him, nobody guarded the toilet.
Unluckily for him, the whole castle had been using it for six months.
You see, a medieval toilet was just a stone seat over a chute, straight down the outside wall.
It's like a bank vault with a steel door, and a doggy door in the back.
His friends followed, set fires, and opened the gates.
Soon after, the last wall fell, and John lost Normandy.
Richard said he'd hold it even if the walls were made of butter.
The walls were fine. It was the toilet.
